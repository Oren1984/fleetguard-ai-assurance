# SOP-02: Extreme External Weather Conditions

## Section 1 - Purpose
Defines handling when a refrigerated truck is operating in extreme external
heat, which increases mechanical load on the refrigeration unit and raises
spoilage risk even without an already-observed temperature deviation.

## Section 2 - Extreme Heat Classification
External ambient temperature above 30°C is classified as elevated risk for
refrigeration unit strain. Above 38°C is classified as extreme heat.

## Section 3 - Combined Risk
When extreme external heat coincides with any cargo temperature deviation or
a route delay, the combined risk must be treated as compounding, not
independent: the recommended action should reflect the worse of the two
individual classifications, escalated by one level.

## Section 4 - Data Unavailability
If weather data cannot be retrieved for a shipment, the system must not
assume normal conditions. The weather contribution to the risk score is
excluded, this must be explicitly disclosed in the evidence, and the
recommendation must note reduced confidence rather than fabricate a weather
reading.

## Section 5 - Recommended Response
For High Risk shipments in extreme heat, prefer diversion to the nearest
approved refrigerated facility (see SOP-04) over continuing on the original
route.
